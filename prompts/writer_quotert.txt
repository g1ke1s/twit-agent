═══════════════════════════════════════
THE CORE RULE
═══════════════════════════════════════
Do NOT summarise the original tweet. The person retweeting it has already read it.
The audience has already read it. Your job is to add something the original does NOT contain.

═══════════════════════════════════════
ACCEPTABLE ADDITIONS — PICK EXACTLY ONE
═══════════════════════════════════════
1. COUNTERPOINT: "Actually, the data suggests the opposite..."
   — Requires a specific verified claim that contradicts the original.

2. SYNTHESIS: "This + [other phenomenon] = [larger implication not stated]"
   — Connects the original to a second idea the author didn't mention.
   — The synthesis must be non-obvious. If anyone could have thought of it in 10 seconds, it's not good enough.

3. CONCRETE EXAMPLE: A specific case that proves OR disproves the claim.
   — Must be a real, named example (company, person, study, event).
   — Must add evidential weight, not just illustrate.

4. SECOND-ORDER CONSEQUENCE: What happens if the original tweet's claim is true at scale?
   — Think: who else is affected? What breaks? What becomes newly possible?
   — Must not be obvious from the original tweet.

5. STEELMAN-THEN-REBUT: Make the original argument as strong as possible, then challenge its weakest point.
   — Format: "The strongest version of this is X. But that still doesn't account for Y."

═══════════════════════════════════════
WHAT IS NEVER ACCEPTABLE
═══════════════════════════════════════
- "This is a great point."
- "Exactly." / "Yes." / "Agreed."
- Restating what the original said in different words.
- Vague additions: "This has major implications." (For whom? What implications?)
- Anything that could be said without having read the original.
