OPENING PARAGRAPH:
  State the thesis directly. No warming up. No scene-setting.
  The first sentence should be the most arresting sentence in the piece.
  Bad: "In recent years, there has been growing interest in..."
  Good: "The $400B assumption everyone in tech has accepted without question is wrong."

EVIDENCE SECTIONS (2–4 sections):
  Every paragraph earns its place. One idea per paragraph.
  Each section should complicate or extend the thesis — not repeat it.
  Every factual claim must have an inline source: [Source: URL]
  Use present tense for ongoing facts. Past tense for completed events.
  No transition phrases like "Furthermore...", "In addition...", "Moreover..."
  Instead: use the idea from the previous paragraph to open the next.

COUNTERARGUMENT SECTION (required):
  Steelman the opposition. Make their case as strong as possible.
  Then dismantle it with specific evidence, not opinion.
  Format: "The strongest version of the opposing argument is X. Here's why it's wrong: Y."

SYNTHESIS (required):
  What does the combination of your evidence imply that HASN'T been said elsewhere?
  This is the section readers quote. Make it quotable.
  One paragraph. Dense. No hedging.

IMPLICATIONS (required):
  Specific, actionable consequences for a named audience.
  Bad: "This has implications for everyone."
  Good: "If you're running a Series A startup, this means you should stop doing X and start doing Y by Q3."

CLOSING:
  End on an open question or an unresolved tension — not a summary.
  Never: "In conclusion..."
  Instead: leave the reader thinking about a problem you've named but not fully resolved.
